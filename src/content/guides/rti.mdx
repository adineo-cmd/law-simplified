## The short version

The Right to Information Act, 2005 gives you a way to ask a public authority for information. It is not a general right to an explanation, an opinion, or a favour. It is a right to records and information that the authority holds.

You do not have to explain why you want it. You do not need a lawyer. You do need to ask a specific question and pay the prescribed fee.

## What you can ask for

Good RTI requests ask for:

- copies of documents, files, notes, and correspondence;
- inspection of records or works;
- details of decisions, approvals, budgets, and expenditure;
- progress reports, inspection reports, and test results;
- the reasons recorded for a decision, if they are in the file;
- the name and designation of the officer who took a decision.

Weak requests ask for:

- opinions (“What do you think about the scheme?”);
- explanations that require the authority to create new information;
- answers to hypothetical questions;
- information that is exempt under the Act.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Ask for the record, not the story">
“Please provide the file noting and the approval letter dated 12 March 2026” is stronger than “Please explain why the project was approved.”
</Callout>

## Who to ask

Every public authority has a Central Public Information Officer (CPIO) or State Public Information Officer (SPIO). File with that officer.

You can file:

- online through the central RTI portal, where the public authority is covered;
- by post or in person at the public authority’s office;
- through the state RTI portal, where available.

If you file with the wrong authority, it should transfer your request to the correct one within five days. Ask for the transfer in writing.

## What it costs

The central application fee is ₹10. State fees and copying charges vary. People below the poverty line are exempt from the application fee, with proof. Additional charges may apply for photocopies or large documents.

Keep the receipt. It is proof that you filed and paid.

## The timeline

- **30 days** is the normal deadline for a reply.
- **48 hours** applies where the information concerns the life or liberty of a person.
- **5 days** is the usual transfer window if the request is sent to the wrong authority.
- If the information is about a third party, the authority may need to give them a chance to object, which can extend the timeline.

## If you get no reply

1. **First appeal.** File within 30 days of the deadline with the first appellate authority in the same public authority. Explain that the CPIO did not reply.
2. **Second appeal.** If the first appeal does not resolve it, file within 90 days with the Information Commission. You can also complain about a refusal, a delay, or an inadequate reply.
3. **Keep the paper trail.** Attach the original request, the fee receipt, the postal proof, and any replies.

## What RTI is not

- It is not a substitute for a court case or an investigation.
- It does not require the authority to answer every question in the form you want.
- It does not override exemptions for national security, personal privacy, commercial confidence, or ongoing investigations.
- It is not a way to harass individual officials. Use it for public information.

## Your next move

1. Write down the exact records you want and the period they cover.
2. Identify the public authority and its CPIO.
3. File the request with the fee and keep the receipt.
4. Note the 30-day deadline.
5. If there is no reply, file the first appeal.
