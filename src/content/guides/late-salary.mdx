## The short version

A late salary is not just a cash-flow problem. It is a legal problem with a paper trail. The first job is to make the trail clean: what was due, when, how much, and what you have already asked for.

Which law applies is not always obvious. The Payment of Wages Act, 1936 covers certain employees and wage ceilings. State Shops and Establishments Acts cover many establishments. The Code on Wages, 2019 was enacted to consolidate these laws, but its commencement and rules vary. A local labour lawyer or the labour department can confirm what fits your job.

## Check the paper first

Before you send an angry message, find:

- your appointment letter and employment contract;
- the clause that says when salary is paid;
- your last three payslips;
- the bank statement showing the missing credit;
- any email or chat where the delay was discussed;
- your attendance or work log, if the employer disputes it.

Write down the exact amount due. “My salary is late” is hard to act on. “₹42,500 for September 2026 was due on 7 October and has not been credited” is a complaint.

## Ask once, in writing

Send an email to HR, your manager, and accounts. Keep it short:

1. The month and amount due.
2. The contractual pay date.
3. The date you last followed up.
4. A request for the exact credit date and the reason for delay.
5. A deadline for a response.

Our [salary delay email](/toolkit#salary-delay) is ready to adapt. If the employer responds with a promise, ask them to confirm the date in writing. If they respond with a threat, do not escalate in the same thread; save it and get advice.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Keep doing your job">
Continuing to work in good faith protects your position. If you stop working without advice, the employer may claim you abandoned the job.
</Callout>

## Know the escalation routes

Depending on your situation, the options can include:

- **The labour department.** Many states have a labour commissioner or labour officer who can receive a complaint, inspect records, and attempt conciliation.
- **The Payment of Wages Authority.** Where the Payment of Wages Act applies, a claim can be filed for unpaid wages and compensation.
- **The civil court.** For larger claims or where the statutory route does not fit, a civil suit for recovery may be possible.
- **Legal aid.** If you qualify, your District Legal Services Authority can help with a lawyer and court fees.

The labour department is often the cheapest first stop. Ask what documents they need and get an acknowledgement of your complaint.

## If you are leaving

Late salary often overlaps with resignation, termination, or a full-and-final settlement. Keep the issues separate:

- **Salary for work already done.** This is a wage claim.
- **Full-and-final settlement.** This can include leave encashment, bonus, gratuity, and notice pay.
- **Experience letter and Form 16.** These are separate obligations; ask for them in writing.

Do not sign a “full and final” settlement that gives up unpaid wages unless you understand exactly what you are settling. If the amount is disputed, ask for a computation.

## Evidence pack

Make a folder with:

- appointment letter and contract;
- payslips and bank statements;
- attendance, shift rosters, or work emails;
- the salary delay email and any replies;
- the labour department complaint and acknowledgement;
- any resignation or termination letter.

If the employer uses a biometric or app-based attendance system, download your records now. Access can disappear when the employment relationship ends.

## When to get a lawyer

Consider legal advice if:

- the amount is large or the delay is repeated;
- the employer is retaliating against you;
- you are facing sexual harassment or discrimination;
- you have been terminated without the contractual process;
- you are asked to sign a settlement you do not understand.
