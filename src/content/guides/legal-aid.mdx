## The short version

If you cannot afford a lawyer, the state is supposed to provide one in certain cases. This is not a charity scheme run by a kind advocate. It is a statutory system under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, delivered through the National Legal Services Authority (NALSA), State Legal Services Authorities (SLSAs), and District Legal Services Authorities (DLSAs).

The system is not perfect. It is underfunded and overworked. But it exists, and it is free. If you qualify, use it.

## Who can get legal aid

The Act and the schemes made under it cover several categories, including:

- women and children;
- members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes;
- persons with disabilities;
- victims of trafficking or forced labour;
- industrial workmen;
- persons in custody;
- victims of disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, or flood;
- persons whose annual income is below the prescribed limit;
- persons in cases where the interests of justice require it.

The exact income limit and documentary proof vary by state. Ask your DLSA what applies in your district.

## What it covers

Legal aid can include:

- legal advice and counselling;
- a lawyer to appear in court;
- drafting of petitions, complaints, and applications;
- help with court fees and other litigation costs;
- certified copies and translation in some cases;
- assistance with mediation and Lok Adalat.

It does not guarantee the outcome you want. It guarantees access to the process.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Ask early">
Legal aid is easier to arrange before a deadline than after one. If you have a court date, a limitation deadline, or a police notice, contact the DLSA immediately and tell them the date.
</Callout>

## How to apply

1. **Find your DLSA.** Search for the District Legal Services Authority in your district, or call the NALSA helpline at 15100.
2. **Ask what documents they need.** Usually identity proof, address proof, income proof, and the case papers.
3. **Write a short summary of the matter.** One page is enough: what happened, what you need, and any deadlines.
4. **Submit the application.** Ask for an acknowledgement or a reference number.
5. **Follow up.** If you do not hear back, call or visit again. Keep a note of who you spoke to.

Some districts have legal aid clinics in courts, jails, schools, and panchayat offices. A clinic can help you draft the application.

## What to bring

- identity proof (Aadhaar, voter ID, passport, or similar);
- address proof;
- income certificate or proof of eligibility;
- court notices, FIR copy, agreement, or other case documents;
- a timeline of events;
- any correspondence with the other side.

If you do not have a document, do not let that stop you from asking. The DLSA can tell you what is essential and what can be added later.

## If you are not eligible

You can still ask for:

- free legal advice at a legal aid clinic;
- help with drafting;
- a referral to a pro bono lawyer or law school clinic;
- mediation or Lok Adalat, which can be cheaper than litigation.

## Your next move

1. Call 15100 or find your DLSA.
2. Write a one-page summary and gather your documents.
3. Submit the legal aid application.
4. Note the acknowledgement and the next date.
5. If the application is delayed, escalate to the SLSA or the NALSA portal.

Eligibility and procedure vary by state and by the type of case. This guide is a starting point; the DLSA is the authority on your application.
</Callout>
