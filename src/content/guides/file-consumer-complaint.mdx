## The short version

A consumer complaint is a paperwork contest before it is an argument contest. The commissions decide mostly on documents: your invoice, your written complaints, the seller's replies, and a clear statement of the remedy you want. The process is designed to be used without a lawyer, and the fees for smaller claims are modest.

The escalation ladder looks like this:

1. Written complaint to the seller with a deadline.
2. National Consumer Helpline (or the sectoral grievance officer — for e-commerce, the rules require one).
3. Complaint before the appropriate Consumer Commission, filed on e-Daakhil.

## Step one: put the seller on notice

Send a written complaint — email is fine — that names the product, the defect or deficiency, the dates, and the remedy you want. Give fifteen days. Keep the delivery or read receipts. This letter is not a legal formality; it is the first exhibit. Commissions routinely ask complainants what they did before filing, and "I sent this letter on this date" is a strong answer.

Use the [consumer complaint letter](law-simplified/toolkit#consumer-complaint) in the toolkit. One remedy per letter. Attach the invoice, warranty, and photographs.

## Step two: the helpline and the grievance officer

Register the dispute on the National Consumer Helpline (call 1915 or use the portal). You get a ticket number, and many large sellers settle at this stage because the ticket feeds their compliance reporting.

If the seller is an e-commerce entity, the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 require it to have a grievance officer who must acknowledge your complaint within 48 hours and dispose of it within one month. Name the grievance officer in your escalation. If the company stalls, that stalling becomes part of your complaint.

## Step three: pick the right commission

Pecuniary jurisdiction under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (as revised in 2021) is decided by the value of goods or services paid plus the compensation claimed:

- **District Commission:** up to ₹50 lakh. File where you live or work, or where the seller carries on business.
- **State Commission:** above ₹50 lakh and up to ₹2 crore.
- **National Commission:** above ₹2 crore.

Also check the time limit: a complaint should generally be filed within **two years** from the date the cause of action arose. If you are close to the limit, file and explain the delay rather than waiting.

## Filing on e-Daakhil

1. Register on e-Daakhil with your mobile number and email.
2. Choose the commission and the case type (consumer complaint).
3. Fill the complaint form: parties, facts in date order, the defect, the relief sought, and the value of the claim.
4. Attach everything as PDFs: invoice, written complaints, replies, photographs, the helpline ticket.
5. Pay the fee online. It is scaled to the claim — ₹100 for claims up to ₹5 lakh, and it never exceeds ₹7,500 even for large claims.
6. Keep the acknowledgement number. The commission will issue a notice to the opposite party and give dates.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Write the relief section carefully">
"Refund of ₹34,999 with compensation of ₹15,000 for harassment and ₹5,000 towards costs" is a relief the commission can grant in one line. "Justice" is not. Be exact about the money.
</Callout>

## What happens after filing

The opposite party is served and typically gets 30 to 45 days to reply. Most commissions push for settlement at the first hearing; mediation is common and faster than an order. If the case proceeds, you file evidence affidavits and written arguments. Many district-level matters are decided within months rather than years — but be realistic: timelines vary widely by commission and backlog.

A default order is possible if the seller does not appear. Enforcement of an order is a separate application — the money does not arrive on its own.

## The trap to avoid

Do not inflate the claim to "teach them a lesson". Compensation follows documented loss and provable harassment. Exaggerated claims damage credibility at the hearing. Claim what you can prove, and attach the proof.

## Your next move

1. Send the written complaint with a 15-day deadline and keep the proof.
2. Register on the National Consumer Helpline and keep the ticket number.
3. Check the two-year limitation and compute your claim value honestly.
4. File on e-Daakhil with every document attached as a named PDF.
5. Attend the first hearing ready to say the remedy in one sentence.

<Callout tone="note" title="This is general information">
Procedure and jurisdiction thresholds change through rules and notifications. Confirm the current fee schedule and requirements on e-Daakhil or with your District Commission before filing.
</Callout>
