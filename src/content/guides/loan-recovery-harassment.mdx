## The short version

Banks and NBFCs can pursue a default — the loan was taken, and it is owed. But the *method* of pursuit is regulated. RBI's guidelines on recovery agents, and the fair-practices codes every regulated lender must adopt, draw a line between recovery and harassment. On the wrong side of that line, the lender — not only the agent — answers to the RBI Ombudsman, and the debt does not grow faster because the calls were ugly.

The rules the calls must follow:

- **Hours:** calls and visits between **8 am and 7 pm** only.
- **Identification:** the agent must produce their ID card and the bank's authorisation letter on demand, and disclose which bank they represent.
- **Conduct:** no intimidation, no humiliation, no misrepresentation of the amount owed or the consequences, no coercion.
- **Privacy:** the bank may contact references or the person who provided the loan's telephone numbers, but shaming you before an employer, neighbour, or social circle is not a permitted method.
- **Choice of channel:** you can request that communication happen in writing, at an agreed address or email.

## The first week: build the record

Treat each call as evidence, not as an event:

1. Note the date, time, phone number, and what was said or threatened.
2. Where lawful and possible, record the call and say on the recording that you are recording it.
3. Save SMS and WhatsApp messages, including the ones that just say "PAY".
4. Note any visit: who came, what they showed, what they said, who witnessed it.

This record does two jobs: it proves violations to the bank and the ombudsman, and it protects you against the classic fabrication — a visit "remembered" differently once a dispute starts.

## The complaint ladder

1. **The bank's grievance cell.** Every regulated lender must have one, with a named nodal officer. Write, don't call: "Calls at 11 pm on 4, 5 and 6 October; an agent visited my office and spoke to my colleagues; threats to inform my employer. This violates RBI's recovery-agent guidelines. Confirm in writing that collection will follow the code." Ask for a complaint number.
2. **The RBI Ombudsman.** If the bank does not reply within 30 days, rejects, or gives an unsatisfactory reply, complain at cms.rbi.org.in. The ombudsman can direct the bank to pay compensation for loss of time, money, and harassment — awards against lenders for recovery misconduct are routine.
3. **The police,** when the conduct crosses into criminality: threats of violence, trespass,forcible entry, seizure of property without authority. An agent's threat to "take the car tonight" on a loan the agent has no right to seize is not recovery; report it.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Do not pay a stranger to make it stop">
Payments must go to the bank's official account or channel — never cash to an agent, never a UPI to a number that "will settle it today". Agents' informal settlements are the second scam on top of the first debt. Verify every demand against the bank's own statement.
</Callout>

## In parallel: negotiate the actual debt

Harassment complaints stop the ugly method; only a repayment plan fixes the situation. Lenders have standing options that agents rarely mention:

- **Restructuring or EMI reduction** for genuine hardship — ask for the bank's restructuring desk, in writing.
- **A settlement** — a lump sum against closure — negotiated in writing, with the closure letter and NOC as the deliverable, not an agent's word.
- **Loan transfer / balance transfer** to another lender, where your credit profile allows.

Put every proposal in writing and keep the trail. "I proposed restructuring on 5 October; the bank replied on 19 October" is also what an ombudsman reads when the bank claims you were uncooperative.

## What agents cannot legally do

- Seize household goods: personal effects and household items are exempt from seizure even under security enforcement.
- Seize a vehicle without due process — repossession under a loan agreement still requires the lender's own procedure, notice, and an inventory; a midnight tow with no paperwork is actionable.
- Arrest you: default on a loan is a civil matter. Threats of arrest for non-payment are misrepresentation — and, where cheating is alleged, only a court decides.
- Publicise your default to shame you: privacy rules apply to lenders, not just to agents.

## Your next move

1. Start the call-and-visit log today, with numbers and recordings.
2. Write to the bank's grievance cell naming the violations and asking for the code to be followed.
3. Complain to the RBI Ombudsman if the bank stalls past 30 days.
4. Make one written repayment proposal — restructuring or settlement — and keep the reply.
5. Report criminal conduct (threats, trespass, seizure) to the police with the log attached.
