## The short version

A road accident is a criminal matter (the FIR), an insurance matter (the claim), and a civil matter (the compensation). The third one — the one people forget — is the Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal, a specialised court created precisely so that accident victims do not need to master civil procedure. Its mandate is speed, and its awards bind insurers to pay.

Who can claim: the victim, or where death results, the legal representatives and dependents. Against whom: the vehicle's driver, its owner, and its insurer — joined together in one application, so the insurer cannot hide behind "sue the driver first".

## Where and when to file

The application goes to the MACT of the area where the claimant resides or carries on business, or where the accident occurred — claimant's choice. Limitation is **six months** from the date of the accident (extendable on sufficient cause, but never plan to test that). The application states the accident's facts, the vehicles and their registration numbers, the injuries or death, the income of the victim, and the compensation claimed with its breakup.

Fee is nominal, and legal aid covers claimants who qualify — accident claims are a standard legal-aid matter.

## What the compensation is made of

Courts compute awards from defined heads:

- **Death:** the deceased's income (minus personal expenses) multiplied by the multiplier matched to age, plus loss of estate, funeral expenses, and — for a homemaker — notional income that courts now assess seriously.
- **Injury:** medical expenses, loss of income during treatment, future loss of earning capacity assessed through disability percentage, pain and suffering, and attendant costs.
- **Structured formulas** under the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 fix minimums — the Second Schedule formulas — and no-fault amounts, but a properly documented claim routinely exceeds the minimums.

The two documents that move the number most: **income proof** (salary slips, returns, business records — or a documented notional income where none exists) and the **disability assessment** (a doctor's disability certificate for lasting injuries).

## Hit and run: the state fund

When the vehicle is untraced, the claim does not die. The Hit and Run Motor Vehicles Accident Compensation Scheme pays fixed amounts from a central fund through the General Insurance Council — filed with the police report, typically through the insurer nominated in your state. The amounts are modest compared to a full MACT award, and if the vehicle is later traced, the MACT claim proceeds normally.

## The process, realistically

1. The police investigation produces the **charge sheet** — its documents (FIR, MLC, spot sketch, mechanical inspection) become the claim's evidence, which is why cooperating with the investigation helps the money claim too.
2. The tribunal summons the insurer to file its reply; the insurer's usual defences are licence issues, drink-driving, or "policy not in force" — each answerable with documents.
3. Evidence is largely affidavits; attendance is required for cross-examination where disputed.
4. Interim relief: the tribunal can award **interim compensation** early, including the statutory no-fault amounts for death and grievous hurt.

<Callout tone="tip" title="The insurer's favourite defence has a short life">
Insurers sometimes argue the driver had no valid licence or was drunk, to escape liability to their own insured. The tribunal's answer, settled by the Supreme Court, is that the victim still gets paid — the insurer pays the award and recovers from the owner where the breach is proved. Do not let that defence delay your claim.
</Callout>

## The documents to assemble now

FIR and final report copy, MLC and all treatment records with bills, the disability certificate when treatment stabilises, income proofs of the victim, the vehicle's RC and insurance particulars (from the police papers), death certificate and legal-heir certificate where death occurred, and the bank details for the award. Claims stall on heirship papers more than on anything else — start the legal-heir certificate early if you must.

## The trap to avoid

Do not settle with the driver or insurer for a small cash amount at the accident site and sign anything called a "final settlement". That signature is the defence's best exhibit before the tribunal. And do not wait for the criminal case to finish — the MACT claim runs independently and should be filed within limitation regardless.

## Your next move

1. Collect the FIR number, vehicle registration numbers, and the investigating officer's details at the scene or station.
2. Preserve every medical document from day one — the file *is* the claim.
3. File the MACT application within six months, joining driver, owner, and insurer.
4. Claim interim compensation and use legal aid for representation.
5. For a hit-and-run, file the scheme claim through the General Insurance Council route while the police search continues.

<Callout tone="note" title="This is general information">
Compensation turns on individual facts — income, injuries, liability disputes. This guide maps the tribunal's machinery; for large claims, a lawyer's eyes on the computation heads usually pays for itself.
</Callout>
