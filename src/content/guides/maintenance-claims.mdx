## The short version

Maintenance is the law's answer to a simple situation: one person in a household controls the money, and the household runs out of it. The statute — Section 125 of the old CrPC, now Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita — lets a magistrate order a monthly allowance, quickly, without waiting for a divorce or a full family-court trial.

Who can claim: a **wife** (including a legally divorced wife who has not remarried), **legitimate and illegitimate minor children** (and adult children unable to support themselves), and **parents** who cannot maintain themselves. The claim runs against any person with sufficient means who neglects or refuses to maintain them.

## It is not only for divorced people

The most common misconception is that maintenance starts when a marriage ends. Section 125's logic is the opposite: the duty to maintain a wife exists inside the marriage. A woman living separately — whether because she was driven out, left to escape cruelty, or the couple is merely estranged — can claim maintenance while still married, so long as she is not living in adultery, refuse unreasonably to live with a husband who offers to maintain her on his usual standard, or the separation is by mutual consent.

That third carve-out matters: "he offered to take me back" is not a defence if the offer ignored the reasons she left. Magistrates look at the substance of the offer, not its existence.

## How much, and decided on what

There is no fixed formula in the statute for Section 125 claims. Courts weigh the husband's income and means against the claimant's needs and standard of living — and the standard of living that matters is the one of the marital household, not bare subsistence. Courts routinely require disclosure: salary slips, Form 16, bank statements, business records. A husband who under-declares income gets his lifestyle read against his paperwork.

**Interim maintenance** can be fixed at an early stage, so the household does not starve while the case runs. Non-payment is enforceable by warrant; a magistrate can also sentence for non-compliance after the amount is due.

## Parallel claims, not either-or

Maintenance can be claimed in multiple forums at once, and this confuses everyone:

- **Section 125 BNSS proceedings** before a magistrate — fast, summary, criminal in procedure.
- **In a divorce or family-court case** — permanent alimony and maintenance under the marriage statutes (Hindu Marriage Act, Special Marriage Act, Muslim law personal statutes).
- **Under the DV Act, 2005** — a protection officer's report can support a maintenance order for an aggrieved woman in a domestic relationship.

Courts adjust rather than duplicate: interim maintenance in one forum is disclosed in the others, and final orders are coordinated. Do not decline a fast forum because another is pending.

## Stridhan is not maintenance

Stridhan — the property, gifts, and money that belong to a woman in her own right — is separate from any maintenance claim and remains hers absolutely. If it is held by in-laws or a husband and refused back, that is recoverable, and the [domestic violence route](law-simplified/guides/domestic-violence) includes recovery orders. Keep proofs: bills, photographs, transfer records for gold and appliances gifted at the wedding.

## The trap to avoid

Do not refuse the offered maintenance outright in anger and record nothing. If the offer is inadequate, say so in writing and explain why — that record is what defeats the "she refused to live with me" defence later. And do not conceal your own income: a maintenance claim is decided on both sides' finances, and exaggeration on one side licenses disbelief on the other.

## Your next move

1. Collect the household's standard-of-living evidence: rent receipts, school fees, medical bills, photographs.
2. Assemble what you know of the earner's income: employer, salary, vehicles, business.
3. Approach the magistrate's court where you live (or the family court if a matrimonial case is already running), or the District Legal Services Authority for free representation.
4. Ask for interim maintenance in the application itself.
5. Keep every order and payment record — arrears are calculated from them.
