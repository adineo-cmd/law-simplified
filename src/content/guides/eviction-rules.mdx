## The short version

There is a legal way to end a tenancy: a valid notice, and then — if the tenant does not leave — an order from a rent authority or civil court, executed with the help of the police if needed. There is also the illegal way: changing the locks while you are at work, cutting the power, removing the water connection, or simply throwing your things into the corridor.

The second way is not a shortcut the landlord is allowed to take. It is called self-help eviction, and it is unlawful everywhere in India, whatever the rent agreement says. An agreement may allow the landlord to terminate a tenancy; it cannot authorise them to be the police, the court, and the bailiff.

## What due process looks like

If the landlord wants the flat back:

1. **A termination notice** per the agreement's notice clause, or reasonable notice where the agreement is silent.
2. **A proceeding before the right forum** — a rent authority in states with rent laws, or a civil court suit for possession and mesne profits elsewhere.
3. **An order**, and only then possession — executed by the court's process, not by the landlord's locksmith.

If you are the one leaving, the same notice logic applies: read the [agreement's notice and lock-in clauses](/guides/rent-agreement) before you decide the moving date.

## The day the locks change

If you come home to changed locks, cut power, or your belongings outside:

1. **Photograph everything** — the lock, the power meter, the belongings, the time. A timestamped video is better.
2. **Call the police (112)** and insist on a complaint or DDR number. Say the words "unlawful lockout" and "wrongful restraint". Even if the police call it a civil matter, the number proves the date and facts.
3. **Do not break back in violently.** If re-entry is safe and peaceful — say, a spare key — take it, and note it. If it means a confrontation, wait for the police or a court order.
4. **Send a written demand the same day**: re-entry, restoration of utilities, and access to your property, within 24 hours.
5. **Escalate to a lawyer or legal aid immediately** — the urgency is real, and a letter on an advocate's letterhead often reopens doors faster than weeks of argument.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Your phone is the courtroom">
In lockout disputes, the winner is usually whoever has the better record. Photos with time and location, the police complaint number, WhatsApp messages, the rent receipts — assemble them the same day, while memory and timestamps are fresh.
</Callout>

## What you can claim

A wrongful lockout is recoverable, not just regrettable:

- **Re-entry** to the premises, through the court if necessary.
- **Damages** for the period you were kept out — the landlord, not you, created the vacancy.
- **Your property** — the landlord has a duty to keep your goods safe, not a right to sell them. An inventory and photos decide what existed.
- **Utilities** — restoring an electricity or water connection they cut.

Where the deposit is also being withheld, run both fights in one letter: see the [security deposit guide](/guides/security-deposit).

## If you are behind on rent

Owing rent does not invite a lockout either. It invites a notice, a quit demand, or a possession proceeding. If the landlord's real grievance is unpaid rent, the fast legal answer is usually to pay or negotiate a payment plan in writing — which also removes their only legitimate lever. If you cannot pay, legal aid and mediation exist precisely for this situation, and "I offered a written payment plan" is a far better exhibit than silence.

## The trap to avoid

Do not stop paying rent as revenge while you fight. Courts look at conduct: a tenant who kept paying and documented everything is a sympathetic figure; a tenant who withheld money and escalated on WhatsApp is not. Pay into the fight, argue the rest.

## Your next move

1. Read the notice and termination clauses of your agreement today.
2. If threatened, start the evidence file before anything happens.
3. If locked out: photographs, police number, same-day written demand.
4. Send a combined demand — re-entry, utilities, deposit, property.
5. Get legal aid or a tenant lawyer involved within days, not weeks.

<Callout tone="note" title="This is general information">
Rent and eviction law is heavily state-specific. The principle — no self-help eviction — is general; the forum and the forms are not. Confirm the local process with your District Legal Services Authority.
</Callout>
