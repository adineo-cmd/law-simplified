## The short version

A police stop is not automatically an arrest. You may be asked for identification, questioned, or checked. You can ask why. You can ask for the officer’s identity. You can inform someone. You can ask for a lawyer.

If you are arrested, the rules change. The police must tell you the grounds, prepare an arrest memo, inform a family member or friend, and produce you before a Magistrate within 24 hours. The D.K. Basu guidelines are the Supreme Court’s checklist for that process.

## If you are stopped on the street

1. **Stay calm and keep your hands visible.** Resist the urge to argue or walk away.
2. **Ask why you are being stopped.** “May I ask why I am being stopped?” is enough.
3. **Ask for identification.** Note the officer’s name, badge number, and police station.
4. **Show identification if required.** You can ask what legal provision requires it.
5. **Do not resist, threaten, or film in a way that escalates the situation.** If you record, do it openly and safely, and know that the officer may object.
6. **Ask if you are free to go.** If you are not, ask whether you are being detained or arrested.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Write it down while it is fresh">
As soon as you can, note the date, time, place, officer names, badge numbers, what was said, and any witnesses. A contemporaneous note is more useful than a reconstructed story.
</Callout>

## If you are detained or arrested

Ask for:

- the grounds of arrest;
- the arrest memo, with the date, time, and signature of a witness;
- permission to inform a family member or friend;
- access to a lawyer;
- a medical examination if you have injuries or feel unwell;
- a copy of the FIR, if one has been registered.

You must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours of arrest. The police cannot hold you indefinitely without that step. If you are not produced, a habeas corpus petition or a complaint to the Magistrate may be appropriate; a legal aid lawyer can help.

You are not obliged to answer questions that may incriminate you. Say that you want to speak to a lawyer. Do not sign statements you have not read or do not understand.

## If you are a woman

The law and Supreme Court guidelines provide additional protections. A woman is generally not to be arrested after sunset and before sunrise, except in exceptional circumstances with the prior permission of a Magistrate. A woman officer should conduct the search, and the arrest should respect modesty and dignity.

If these protections are violated, note the facts and contact a lawyer or the State Human Rights Commission.

## At the police station

- Ask for the FIR number and a copy.
- Ask for the name and designation of the investigating officer.
- Do not sign blank papers.
- Do not pay any money to “settle” a criminal matter without legal advice.
- If you are a victim, ask for a free copy of the FIR and information about the investigation.
- If you cannot afford a lawyer, ask for legal aid.

## Complaints against the police

If you believe the police acted unlawfully, you can:

- complain to the Station House Officer or the Superintendent of Police;
- approach the Magistrate;
- complain to the State Human Rights Commission or the National Human Rights Commission;
- contact a legal aid lawyer or a civil liberties organisation.

Keep copies of every complaint and acknowledgement. A complaint without a record is easy to lose.

## Your next move

1. Stay calm and ask why you are being stopped.
2. Note names, badge numbers, time, and place.
3. If arrested, ask for the grounds, the arrest memo, a lawyer, and family contact.
4. Ask for the FIR copy and the investigating officer’s details.
5. If you cannot afford a lawyer, call 15100 or your DLSA.

<Callout tone="note" title="This is general information">
Police powers and procedures are fact-specific. This guide explains general rights and Supreme Court guidelines; it is not legal advice on your encounter.
</Callout>
