## The short version

An income tax notice is a letter with a section number and a deadline. The section number is everything: it tells you what the department thinks, what it can do, and how many days you have. Most notices are automated data-matching letters, not accusations — and the response is a form on the e-filing portal, not a court appearance.

Before anything else, log in to the portal and download your **AIS (Annual Information Statement)** and **TIS (Taxpayer Information Summary)**. These aggregates — from banks, brokers, mutual funds, registrars, and employers — are what the department matched you against. Nine of ten notices die in the AIS reconciliation.

## The notices, by what they want

**1. Intimation u/s 143(1)** — not really a notice: the processing summary of your return. A refund, a small demand, or "no change". Compare the columns with what you filed; a demand here can be a computation mismatch, corrected by a rectification request online.

**2. Defective return u/s 139(9)** — your return has a formal defect: a mismatched PAN-Aadhaar, missing particulars, an ITR form that doesn't fit your income. Respond within **15 days** by filing a corrected return or explaining the defect on the portal. Silence voids the return as if never filed.

**3. Inquiry u/s 142(1)** — "provide documents/details" — often for an older year or a high-value transaction. The deadline is stated in the notice. Respond with what is asked, in the format asked, through the portal.

**4. Scrutiny u/s 143(2)** — your return is selected for examination. This is the serious one: file the response with full documentation, meet every date, and engage a CA for anything above a simple reconciliation. Scrutiny ends in an assessment or amendment order — appealable if you disagree (first appeal to the Joint Commissioner/Appeals within 30 days).

**5. Escaped income u/s 148** — "income has escaped assessment" for a past year. This one starts a clock on you: respond to the notice, file the return for that year, and get professional help — the department has reopened the year, and the battle is about what it can reopen.

**6. Demand notice u/s 156** — a computed tax demand. Pay, or dispute with reasons within the stated window; interest accrues on silence.

**7. Refund adjustment u/s 245** — the department plans to set off your refund against an old demand. You can object on the portal with reasons before the set-off — this is the notice people lose refunds to by not opening.

<Callout tone="tip" title="The deadline lives in the notice, and it is real">
"Respond within 15/30 days" is not decorative. Miss a 142(1) and the assessment proceeds on the department's best judgment; miss a 139(9) and the return is void. Calendar the deadline the day the notice arrives — and note that notices are served to your registered email and the portal's "e-proceedings" tab, which nobody checks.
</Callout>

## How to respond properly

1. **Download the notice** from the portal's e-proceedings tab (not just your inbox) and note the section, the year, and the deadline.
2. **Reconcile against AIS/TIS, Form 26AS, and your own records.** The mismatch story — "the ₹4.2L is my EPF withdrawal, taxed at source, reflected here" — is usually answerable with two documents.
3. **Respond on the portal, in writing.** Every submission gets an acknowledgement number; collect them.
4. **Pay what is genuinely due** — disputing the wrong items while owing the right ones costs interest and credibility.
5. **Escalate through the appeal machinery where you disagree** — the CIT(A) first appeal, then the tribunal. Appeal deadlines are short (30 days from the order), and they run whether or not you are ready.

## Where the mismatches come from

The recurring culprits, in order: savings-bank interest not reported; FD interest under TDS reported by the bank but not filed; stock and F&O turnover mistaken for income (turnover ≠ profit — the AIS shows turnover, your ITR shows gains); mutual fund redemptions read as income; employer corrections after the Form 16 snapshot; and rent or property transactions matched against nothing you filed. Knowing the genre of error is half the response.

## The trap to avoid

Do not file a "revised" return to fix a scrutiny year without advice — revisions have windows and consequences, and an unnecessary revision can reopen what was closed. And do not respond by email or phone alone: the portal's record is the only record the department reads.

## Your next move

1. Open the e-proceedings tab today — check for notices you have not seen.
2. Download AIS and TIS and reconcile them against your filed returns, this week.
3. For any live notice: note section, year, deadline — and respond on the portal before it.
4. Pay undisputed demands to stop interest; dispute the rest in writing.
5. For scrutiny and 148 notices, take a CA into the room before the first response, not after.
