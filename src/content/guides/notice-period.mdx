## The short version

Resignation disputes come in two flavours that feel identical and are legally different: the notice period (a contractual obligation you negotiate) and the final settlement (money and documents you have already earned). Employers have leverage on the first. On the second, the law is mostly on your side — earned wages cannot be held hostage to a disputed notice, and your statutory dues follow you, not your manager's signature.

## Read your contract before you draft your resignation

Three clauses decide the exit:

- **Notice period:** typically 30 to 90 days. Some contracts allow **notice pay** — buying out the notice — some forbid it. If the clause is silent, the buy-out is negotiable, not a right and not a crime.
- **Garden leave and non-competes:** you can be asked to not join a competitor during notice. Enforceable non-competes *after* employment ends are nearly impossible under Indian law — restraint of trade under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act — but non-solicitation and confidentiality clauses are commonly enforced, so read them.
- **Recovery clauses:** training bonds and relocation recoveries must be reasonable and proportionate; a recovery that exceeds actual documented loss invites a challenge.

## Resign properly, on the record

A resignation letter is a legal instrument, not a goodbye note. It should say: the date, that you are resigning, your last working day computed from the notice clause, and a request for the full and final settlement process to begin. Send it to the official channel — HR email, not just a chat to your manager — and keep the sent copy.

After that, work the notice professionally. A documented, cooperative notice period is the cheapest insurance for everything below: an employer arguing abandonment has nothing to say against an employee whose attendance and handover records are clean.

## The full and final settlement

Your FnF includes: salary for days worked, encashed earned leave per the contract and applicable rules, bonus or incentives per their terms, and expense reimbursements. Indian labour law does not set a single nationwide FnF deadline — it follows your contract and state rules — but an indefinite delay is not a lawful answer. Companies' own policies typically promise 30 to 90 days; that promised date is your deadline. Put it in writing:

> "As per the company's separation policy dated [X], the FnF settlement is due within 45 days. My last working day was [date]. Please process the settlement and share the statement by [date + 15 days]."

If it stalls, the escalation is [the late-salary playbook](law-simplified/guides/late-salary): a written demand, then the labour commissioner or the payment-of-wages machinery for wages due. Withholding earned salary because "the exit interview is pending" or "the laptop is not returned" is not a lawful offset — dues and recoveries are settled in the FnF statement, itemised, not held back silently.

## The relieving and experience letter

These documents gate your next job, and refusing to issue them because of a dispute is a classic pressure tactic. They are ordinary documents of employment: the relieving letter confirms your tenure and last day; the experience letter summarises your role. You can demand both in writing. If an employer refuses, the demand letter — plus the labour machinery for wages — usually moves them, and your records (offer letter, salary slips, bank credits, the resignation acceptance) can establish employment for background verification even in the worst case.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Do not 'abscond' by accident">
Leaving without formal resignation, or ignoring emails after you stop showing up, converts a settlement into an abandonment case — and hands the employer a counter-narrative. If you have already left and the employer is stonewalling, do not go silent: keep sending dated written demands from your personal email so the record shows you were responsive and they were not.
</Callout>

## PF, gratuity and the money that is already yours

- **Provident fund** sits in your UAN, not in your employer's custody. After exit, your previous employer marks your exit date, and you can transfer the corpus to the new UAN-linked account or withdraw per EPFO rules. The employer's role is ministerial — marking the exit — and EPFO's grievance portal (EPFiGMS) exists when they do not do it.
- **Gratuity** accrues on continuous service of five years, at 15 days' wages per completed year (for most employees). It is a statutory right under the Payment of Gratuity Act, and it does not depend on a "good exit".
- **Salary slips and Form 16** for every worked year must be provided; they are your income proof for loans, visas, and the next employer's verification.

## If they threaten to "blacklist" or defame

Background verification is a business process; sending false or defamatory statements about you to a new employer is not. A calm letter noting that misstatements to third parties will be met with a defamation claim, from an advocate's letterhead if needed, is the proportionate answer to that threat.

## Your next move

1. Read the notice, buy-out, and recovery clauses before resigning.
2. Resign in writing through the official channel, with dates computed from the contract.
3. Demand the FnF statement, relieving letter, and experience letter in writing, with the policy's own deadline.
4. Confirm your exit date in the UAN portal and file an EPFiGMS grievance if it never gets marked.
5. Keep every sent email — the paper trail is the whole case.
