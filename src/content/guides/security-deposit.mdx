## The short version

A security deposit is not a tip for the landlord. It is money held against unpaid rent or damage, and the terms should be in your written agreement.

But there is no single national statute that says “every landlord must refund within 30 days” or “no deposit may exceed two months’ rent”. The Model Tenancy Act, 2021 is a template for states; it is not automatically the law in your city. Your agreement and the state rent law that covers your building are the first things to read.

## Read the agreement like a checklist

Find these clauses and write down the answers:

- **How much is the deposit?** Is it one month, two months, or something else?
- **When is it refundable?** On handover, after a notice period, or after the final electricity bill?
- **What can be deducted?** Unpaid rent, utilities, damage, cleaning, painting, or “any other charges”?
- **What is normal wear and tear?** The agreement may not define it. That is exactly why photographs matter.
- **What notice must you give?** A lock-in period can make the deposit a negotiation, not a refund.
- **Who holds the deposit?** The landlord, a broker, or a company? Get the name right in your letter.

If the agreement says the deposit is refundable “after deduction of all dues” but does not require bills, you can still ask for an itemised statement. A deduction without a basis is a deduction you can dispute.

A dated video walkthrough, meter readings, and a signed inventory are the cheapest insurance you will ever buy. Do it before you give up possession.
</Callout>

## Document the handover

On the day you move out:

1. Walk through every room and record a video with the date visible.
2. Photograph the meter readings, the condition of walls, floors, fixtures, and appliances.
3. Note the inventory: keys, remotes, furniture, appliances, parking access.
4. Get the landlord or their representative to sign a handover note, or send the note by email the same day.
5. Keep the last rent receipt and utility bills.

If the landlord refuses to sign, send the video and a written summary by email. The point is not to force agreement; it is to create a contemporaneous record.

## Ask in writing

Send a short letter that refers to the agreement, the handover date, the deposit amount, and the refund clause. Ask for either the full amount or an itemised deduction with bills. Give a clear deadline — 15 days is reasonable.

Our [security deposit refund letter](/toolkit#deposit-demand) is ready to adapt. Keep it polite and specific. “Please refund my deposit” is weaker than “Please refund ₹80,000 by 15 November or send an itemised statement with receipts”.

## If the landlord deducts

Ask three questions:

1. What exactly was damaged?
2. When was it damaged, and how?
3. What is the bill or receipt for the repair?

Normal wear and tear — faded paint, minor scuffs, a worn gasket — is not damage. A landlord cannot turn the deposit into a renovation fund. If the deduction is for painting, ask whether the agreement requires you to repaint and whether the amount is proportionate to the flat’s age and condition.

If the landlord claims unpaid rent, compare their statement with your bank records and receipts. If they claim utility dues, ask for the utility bill in their name or the transfer document.

## Escalate

The right forum depends on your state and the amount:

- **Mediation or a rent authority.** Some states have rent authorities or mediation mechanisms, especially where the Model Tenancy Act has been adopted. Ask your local legal aid clinic what exists in your city.
- **Civil court.** A suit for recovery of the deposit can be filed, but the cost and time may exceed the amount. A lawyer can tell you whether the claim is worth it.
- **Legal aid.** If you qualify, your District Legal Services Authority can provide a lawyer and help with court fees.

Do not withhold the keys or stop paying rent as a bargaining chip without advice. That can shift the legal position against you.

## The Model Tenancy Act, explained

The Model Tenancy Act, 2021 was drafted to make renting more predictable. It suggests written agreements, a rent authority, and a time-bound deposit refund. But “model” means exactly that: states must adopt it, and they can change it. Do not cite it as binding law unless your state has notified it.

## Your next move

1. Find the deposit and refund clauses in your agreement.
2. Assemble the handover video, photos, meter readings, and payment proof.
3. Send the written refund request with a deadline.
4. If there is a deduction, ask for an itemised statement and bills.
5. Ask your District Legal Services Authority or a local rent lawyer about the right forum.
