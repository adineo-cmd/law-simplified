## The short version

Cyber fraud is a race. The money can move through multiple accounts in minutes. The first hour is not about writing a perfect complaint; it is about stopping the next transaction and creating a record that the bank and the police can act on.

There are three calls to make, in this order: the helpline, your bank, and the online portal.

## Minute 0–10: call 1930

Call the National Cyber Crime Helpline at **1930**. Give the operator:

- your name and phone number;
- the date and time of the transaction;
- the amount;
- your bank and account ending;
- the transaction ID/UTR;
- the UPI ID, account number, phone number, email, or website used by the fraudster;
- a one-line description of the scam.

Write down the complaint or acknowledgement number. If you cannot get through, keep trying while you do the next step.

## Minute 10–30: call your bank

Call your bank’s fraud or customer-care line. Ask them to:

1. Block the card, UPI handle, or net-banking access involved.
2. Register a dispute for the transaction.
3. Try to freeze the beneficiary account, if the bank can act.
4. Give you a complaint/reference number and an email confirmation.

Then send a written email with the subject line: **“Unauthorised/fraudulent transaction — [date] — ₹[amount] — UTR [number]”**. Attach the transaction screenshot and the police/helpline acknowledgement.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Do not delay for a perfect story">
Banks and police can work with an incomplete complaint. They cannot work with a complaint you send tomorrow. Report first, refine later.
</Callout>

## Minute 30–60: file online

Go to **cybercrime.gov.in** and file a complaint. Keep these ready:

- screenshots of the transaction, chat, call log, and the fake profile/website;
- the UPI ID, bank account, phone number, or email used by the fraudster;
- your bank statement showing the debit;
- any voice message or call recording;
- the 1930 acknowledgement, if you have one.

The portal will give you an acknowledgement number. Save it and share it with your bank.

## Preserve the evidence

- Do not delete the chat, email, or call log.
- Do not factory-reset your phone.
- Take screenshots with the date and time visible.
- Save the original files, not just forwarded copies.
- If the fraud happened through a website, save the URL and a full-page screenshot.
- If you recorded a call, keep the original file. Check your state’s rules on recording before sharing it widely.

## The second scam

After a fraud, you may be contacted by someone claiming to be a “cyber cell officer”, a “recovery agent”, or a bank official who can get your money back for a fee. Treat unsolicited recovery offers as a second scam.

- Do not pay an upfront fee.
- Do not share OTPs, passwords, or screen-sharing access.
- Do not click a “refund” link sent by someone who contacted you first.
- Verify the caller through the official bank or police number you find yourself.

## If the bank does not resolve it

Ask for the written complaint number and the bank’s final response. If the bank delays or rejects the dispute without a clear reason, you can escalate under the RBI’s Integrated Ombudsman Scheme. The portal and the bank should give you the relevant details.

## Your next move

1. Call 1930.
2. Call your bank and block the access.
3. File at cybercrime.gov.in.
4. Email the bank with the acknowledgement.
5. Preserve the evidence and follow up with the reference numbers.

<Callout tone="note" title="This is general information">
Recovery depends on the facts and timing. This guide is a practical checklist, not a guarantee that the money will be recovered.
</Callout>
