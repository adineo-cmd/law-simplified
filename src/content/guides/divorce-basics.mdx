## The short version

Indian family law does not do "irreconcilable differences". Every divorce runs on statutory grounds: either both spouses jointly seek **divorce by mutual consent** under the statutory route, or one spouse proves a fault ground — cruelty, desertion, adultery, conversion, mental disorder, communicable disease, or presumption of death. The court's real work is the ancillary relief: custody, maintenance or alimony, and the division of the household's life.

Which marriage statute applies depends on the religion of the parties — the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 covers Hindus, Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs; the Special Marriage Act, 1954 covers civil and inter-faith marriages; the Divorce Act, 1869 for Christians; Muslim personal law and the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939 for Muslims; the Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, 1936 for Parsis. The machinery below is common enough to describe once.

## The mutual consent route

This is the route for couples who agree the marriage is over and can agree — or negotiate — the terms. The steps:

1. **A joint petition** stating the marriage has broken down, the date the parties began living separately (most statutes require one year of separation), and the agreed terms: custody and visitation, maintenance or a one-time settlement, return of stridhan and articles.
2. **The "first motion"** — both spouses record their statements before the court.
3. **The statutory pause.** The court gives six months (up to eighteen) before the "second motion". The Supreme Court in *Amardeep Singh* confirmed this cooling period can be **waived** where the separation has already been long (typically 18 months or more), all issues are settled, and the court is satisfied the settlement is genuine.
4. **The second motion** and the decree.

What makes it fast or slow is almost never the law — it is whether the terms are actually settled. A memorandum of understanding on money, custody, and articles, exchanged before filing, is what converts six months of litigation into two court dates.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Put every term in the petition itself">
The decree's enforceability tracks the petition's terms. "Wife to receive ₹25 lakh by four quarterly instalments, first on 1 April; husband to permit overnight visitation on alternate weekends" is enforceable. "Both parties to settle amicably" is a quarrel scheduled for later.
</Callout>

## The contested route

One spouse wants out, the other does not (or the terms are not agreed), so the petitioner proves a ground:

- **Cruelty** — the most-used ground, and deliberately broad: physical or mental. Sustained humiliation, false accusations, denial of the marital household, alcohol-fuelled conduct — courts read cruelty from patterns and records, not single meltdowns. Wedding-day photographs of a happy couple do not defeat two years of documented abuse.
- **Desertion** — one spouse abandons the other for the statutory continuous period (two years under the Hindu Marriage Act) without cause and without consent.
- **Adultery** — a ground for divorce (it was decriminalised in 2018, but remains a matrimonial ground).

Evidence decides these cases: messages, emails, financial records, medical records, witnesses. Public posturing — the social media anniversaries — matters less than the private record.

## Custody: welfare, not scorekeeping

Courts decide custody on the child's welfare — emotional and physical, education, stability, and the child's own preference (given weight as the child matures, not decisive before adolescence). Common arrangements: physical custody with one parent and visitation to the other, joint custody in various forms, and interim visitation fixed early so the child's routine does not become a bargaining chip. The parent who files first does not win; the parent who can show a stable, safe, child-centred arrangement does.

## Money: maintenance, alimony, and property

- **Maintenance pending litigation** keeps the dependent spouse and children supported while the case runs — ask for it early.
- **Permanent alimony** at decree is decided on income, earning capacity, the standard of living during the marriage, ages and health, and the child's needs. There is no arithmetic formula; disclosure drives the number.
- **Property** follows title unless agreed otherwise — there is no automatic community property in India. A home in one spouse's name is legally theirs, which is exactly why settlements trade money, articles, and stridhan return for a consent divorce. Stridhan remains the woman's absolute property throughout.

## The paperwork to start assembling now

Marriage certificate or photographs of the ceremony, address proofs, income proofs of both spouses (salary slips, returns, bank statements), the child's birth certificate and school records, the inventory of stridhan and household articles, and the incident record if cruelty is a ground. Half of family-court delay is parties assembling, one summons at a time, what a folder would have contained on day one.

## The trap to avoid

Do not file a contested case "to teach a lesson" and do not sign a consent memorandum you have not priced. The first is the most expensive revenge available; the second is a lifetime instalment plan. Both are avoided the same way: decide your floor — money, custody, articles — before you walk into the lawyer's office, and write it down.

## Your next move

1. Decide the route honestly: are the terms negotiable within weeks, or is this contested?
2. Build the folder above — for both routes it is the same folder.
3. Consult the family court's mediation cell or a lawyer; use legal aid if fees are the barrier.
4. In consent cases, put every term in the joint petition and ask about waiver of the cooling period.
5. Keep every order — interim maintenance and visitation orders are enforced through the same court.
