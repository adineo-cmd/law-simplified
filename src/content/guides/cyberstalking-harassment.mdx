## The short version

Online harassment cases are won and lost on the evidence file, not on the force of the complaint. The offences exist: **stalking** (repeatedly following or contacting a woman despite clear disinterest, or monitoring her internet use), **criminal intimidation** (threats of injury or reputation harm), **insulting modesty** through words or gestures, and the IT Act's provisions on privacy violations and obscene or sexually explicit electronic content. The pattern matters: one rude message is a report; a month of them is a case.

## The evidence file comes first

Before any complaint — before you even block:

1. **Full screenshots with the URL, handle, timestamps, and share/reply counts.**
2. **Export the chats** where the app allows it; keep the originals on the device.
3. **A one-page chronology** — dates, what was sent, what you did (ignored, blocked, replied once).
4. **The account trail** — every handle they use; harassers rotate accounts, and the links between them (same photos, same typos, same threats) prove continuity.
5. **Preserve, do not delete.** Deactivating your account or wiping chats to "move on" is the single most common way victims erase their own case.

Only after preservation: block, mute, and lock down — privacy settings, close friends lists, and an audit of what your public profiles reveal (schedules, addresses, workplaces).

## Where to report, in parallel

- **cybercrime.gov.in** — the national portal; the "report other cybercrime" category covers harassment and threats, with a "report anonymously" option and, for women, a dedicated "report related to women" stream with priority handling. You receive an acknowledgement number — that number is what a follow-up with the police stands on.
- **The local cyber cell or police station.** Every city now has one; complaints can often be filed at your own station and routed. Carry the chronology and the screenshots printed.
- **1930**, when the harassment is attached to fraud or financial threats.
- **181 / 112**, for immediate safety concerns; One Stop Centres help women document and file.

For women complainants, the BNS stalking provision is specifically worded; men facing harassment still have criminal intimidation, obscenity, and extortion provisions — the machinery is the same even where the section differs.

## What the platforms can and cannot do

Platforms can: remove content, suspend or ban accounts, preserve records on legal request, and hand over information to law enforcement through the proper process. They cannot: reveal the person behind an account to you directly, or act on what they cannot verify. So report on-platform **and** file the police complaint — the police channel is the one that unlocks identity data.

For impersonation and fake profiles, platforms act fastest; report through both the in-app tool and the grievance officer, with the same evidence file attached.

## The safety layer, before the legal layer

- Tighten every account: two-factor authentication, an email the harasser does not know, login alerts.
- Tell two people you trust what is happening — witnesses to the pattern, and people who check on you.
- If threats mention your home, workplace, or family, treat that as an escalation: file with the police the same day and mention the specific threat verbatim.
- At work or campus, a written note to HR/warden creates a record and often triggers institutional duties.

## The trap to avoid

Do not negotiate with the harasser through a friend, and do not post publicly about them while the case is pending — counter-posts become evidence of a mutual fight and hand the defence a narrative. The quiet path is also the strong one: file, preserve, and let the numbers do the talking.

## Your next move

1. Build the evidence file today — screenshots, exports, chronology, handles.
2. Preserve first; block after.
3. File on cybercrime.gov.in and at the local cyber cell; keep every acknowledgement number.
4. Report each account and post on-platform through the grievance route.
5. Escalate any specific threat to the police immediately, quoting it verbatim.
